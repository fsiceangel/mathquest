# Counting chapter 12: full variant difficulty review

2026-09-22: all 62 base stems and **186 variant stems** compared. Inspected inverse coefficient/row searches, parity tables, overlapping identities, bounded subset sums and all-column occurrence arguments. No pixel-level or original textbook comparison is claimed.

Many apparent difficulty jumps are real reverse tasks: recovering a row from an internal coefficient, collapsing weighted identities and then recovering an index, or processing sixteen parity rows. Explicit overrides preserve those L3/L4 demands. Directly supplied parent sums, child-minus-parent differences, and mirrored positions were lowered where the inverse task disappeared.

The lesson explicitly states that odd- and even-sized subsets each number half the total. Therefore the base direct alternating sum and direct even-size question in 12.5, and challenge #5's direct odd-position sum, were corrected to L1. Matching direct variants use L1 too; fixing included elements, excluding classes of elements, or recovering the row still requires separate steps. Parity subset variants replacing symbolic alternating sums use bridge scope; formal identities remain extension. This corrects an actual taught-prerequisite mismatch rather than changing levels to close a gap.

All variant stems read; nontrivial solution routes inspected; not every answer independently proved. Large inverse-search solutions still depend on monotonicity or an adequate search bound, rather than a single successful numerical guess. Empirical student calibration remains outstanding.
