Read `notes/variation-spec.md` first. This chapter is the biggest one: five sections of $10$,
plus $12$ challenge and $10$ worksheet items — $72$ slots, $216$ pinned variations. Every
KEY below was computed exactly in node with rational arithmetic: full sample-space
enumeration (dice grids, coin/spinner sequences, ordered and unordered draws, digit

the §8.6 multi-stage games. Do not retarget a pin; if your own value disagrees, STOP and
report. Answer indices are derived from the base: v1 = (b+1)%4, v2 = (b+3)%4,
v3 = (b+2)%4. Worksheet items are free response (no indices); their keys are LaTeX
strings, e.g. `'$\\frac{13}{45}$'`.

This chapter’s repair history is duplication, so originality is the first rule here, not
the last. A variation must not reuse a base stem’s objects *and* its question together,
must not be the base with one number changed, and must not restate a learn example.

**Conventions every stem must state.** Dice are fair and independent, and two dice are
distinguishable ("one red and one blue") whenever order could matter. Coins are fair
unless a weight is given, and flips are independent. Spinner sectors are equal unless the
stem gives the sizes or angles. Draws say **with replacement** or **without replacement**,
and say whether items come out one at a time (order matters) or **at the same time**
(unordered set, every set equally likely). Every repeated-trial stem says the trials are
independent. Every game says exactly what stops it — the winning total, the number of
misses, the required lead — and what happens on a wasted turn. "At random from $1$ through
$n$" means every value equally likely.
