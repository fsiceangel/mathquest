the objects directly (position sets, permutations of the relevant cards or people, dice
grids, colored subsets, stopped game sequences; the turn-game shares were confirmed by
geometric partial sums as well). Do not retarget a pin; if your own count disagrees, STOP
and report. Answer indices are derived from the base: v1 = (b+1)%4, v2 = (b+3)%4,
v3 = (b+2)%4. Worksheet items are free response (no indices); their keys are LaTeX
strings, e.g. `'$\\frac{13}{63}$'`.

Every variation must reward the chapter’s viewpoints — symmetry, ignoring outcomes that
cannot decide the question, reframing a game as an equivalent simpler one, and choosing
the sample space — over grinding, and each solution’s two routes should be the clever way
and the grind (or two different clever ways), agreeing at the end.

Conventions to state in every stem: "standard deck" = $52$ cards, shuffled so every order
is equally likely; marble/ticket draws say with or without replacement and whether the
items come out one at a time or together ("at the same time" = unordered set, every set
equally likely); every stopping rule says exactly what stops the process and what happens
on a wasted spin/roll; splits into teams/groups say the sizes and "every split equally
likely"; dice are fair and independent; people are all different.
