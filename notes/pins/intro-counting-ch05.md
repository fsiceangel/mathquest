# Pins — intro-counting ch05 (More With Combinations)

Read `notes/variation-spec.md` first. Every KEY below was brute-forced in node: grid keys by
enumerating **every** monotone path on the exact grid and filtering (the same enumerator
reproduced all ten §5.2 base answers `20 21 40 60 17 18 54 66 63 19` before any variation
was designed); committee keys by enumerating subsets; division keys by enumerating set
partitions; distribution keys by enumerating full assignments or compositions. Do not
retarget a pin; if your own count disagrees, STOP and report.

Answer indices are derived from the base: `v1 = (b+1)%4`, `v2 = (b+3)%4`, `v3 = (b+2)%4`.
Worksheet items are free response (no indices); their keys are LaTeX strings, e.g. `'$28$'`.

Every grid variation copies the `grid()` helper from `ch05.js` verbatim into its variation
file. A pin that names a grid gives it in helper form, e.g. `grid(6, 3)` or
`grid(5, 4, { closed: [[2, 1], [3, 3]] })`; corners are `[right, up]` from $A$.

**Conventions to state in every stem.** Grid: "$A$ is the bottom-left corner, $B$ the
top-right, every step goes one block right or one block up along the streets" (a grid $w$
blocks wide has $w+1$ corners along the bottom). Committee: people are all different, a
committee is an unordered set, "unnamed teams" means two divisions that swap the teams are
the same division. Distribution: say **identical vs different items**, **labeled vs
unlabeled containers**, and **whether a container may be empty** — every stem, no exceptions.
