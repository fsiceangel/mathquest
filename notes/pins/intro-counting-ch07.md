Read `notes/variation-spec.md` first. Every KEY below was brute-forced in node by enumerating the
sample space directly (marble/token/card sets, letter arrangements, dice and spinner grids,
committee subsets, team splits, digit permutations), and every base key was recomputed the
same way as a cross-check. Do not retarget a pin; if your own count disagrees, STOP and
report. Answer indices are derived from the base: v1 = (b+1)%4, v2 = (b+3)%4, v3 = (b+2)%4.
Worksheet items are free response (no indices); their keys are LaTeX strings, e.g.
`'$\\frac{13}{55}$'`, and the reversed items’ integer keys are written `'$16$'`.

This is the chapter where probability is introduced, so the variations must stay inside the
chapter’s tools: name the equally likely outcomes, count the favorable ones, reduce. Nothing
here may use conditional probability, independence chains across stages, expected value, or
geometric probability — those belong to later chapters.

Conventions to state in every stem: a die is a fair standard six-sided die unless the stem
says otherwise, and dice/coins/spins are independent; spinner sectors are equal, and the
label on every sector is given; marbles/tiles/cards drawn "at the same time" means an
unordered set with every set equally likely; a draw of more than one item always says
whether it is with or without replacement; letters of a word go on identical tiles, one
letter per tile, and repeated letters are treated as identical when arrangements are counted
("every distinct arrangement equally likely"); people, books, and cards are all different
unless stated identical; "at least"/"exactly"/"more than" are always spelled out.
